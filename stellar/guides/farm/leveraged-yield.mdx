Leveraged yield means borrowing from Vanna and deploying the loan into a Farm pool, so you earn on more than your own capital. On Stellar you do it in two steps, Margin then Farm, or ask [Copilot](/stellar/guides/copilot) to plan both.

## Open a position

<Steps>
  <Step title="Deposit and borrow">
    On **Margin → Leverage your Assets**, deposit collateral and borrow the token the pool needs, for example XLM or BLUSDC for Blend, or SoUSDC for the Soroswap XLM / SoUSDC pool. See [Borrow](/stellar/guides/margin/borrow).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Farm it">
    Open the pool on **Farm** and supply or add liquidity from the margin account. On Blend, **Source** shows that borrowed funds are used first. See [Blend](/stellar/guides/farm/blend-pools) or [LP Pools](/stellar/guides/farm/amm-liquidity).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the account">
    On Margin, **Borrowed money is in** now shows the amount in **Farm**. Watch the health factor and the Liquidation buffer.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Does it pay?

Your net yield is roughly:

```text
farm APY × amount farmed − borrow APY × amount borrowed
```

Both rates are variable. Compare the pool's supply APY (Farm) with the asset's **Borrow APY** (Earn) and your **Net Borrow Rate** (Margin) before you start. On testnet, rates can swing a lot.

<Warning>
Borrowing XLM to supply XLM to Blend cancels out XLM price moves on that leg. Borrowing USDC to farm XLM does not: you are now long XLM with leverage, and an XLM drop lowers your health factor.
</Warning>

## Unwind

<Steps>
  <Step title="Exit the farm">
    Withdraw from Blend or remove liquidity. Tokens return to the margin account.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Swap if needed">
    If you got back a different token than you owe, [swap](/stellar/guides/trade/spot-swap) for the debt asset.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Repay">
    [Repay](/stellar/guides/margin/repay) each loan in its own asset.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Withdraw">
    [Transfer](/stellar/guides/margin/transfer-collateral) what's left to your wallet.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Each step is its own transaction. If one fails, the earlier ones stay done; check your balances before retrying.
