**Lending Pool** sits at the center - it holds the asset, tracks all deposits and borrows, and computes interest. The other three contracts are its dependencies:

| Component | Role |
|---|---|
| **vToken Contract** | Mints LP shares on deposit, burns them on withdrawal |
| **Rate Model** | Returns the current borrow rate per second, based on utilization |
| **Account Manager** | The only address allowed to call `lend_to()` - no direct user borrows |
| **Risk Engine** | Checks account health before Account Manager approves any borrow |

## Deposits & Exchange Rate

When an LP deposits, the pool mints **vTokens** - shares that represent their portion of the pool.

The number of vTokens minted is:

```
vTokens = deposit_amount × total_vToken_supply / total_pool_balance
```

On the **first ever deposit**, total supply and balance are both zero - so the rate defaults to 1:1. After that, every new deposit gets fewer vTokens per unit because the existing vTokens are already worth more (interest has accrued into the pool balance).

When an LP redeems:

```
USDC returned = vTokens_burned × total_pool_balance / total_vToken_supply
```

The LP gets back more than they deposited - the difference is their share of all the interest the pool collected while they held vTokens.

## Borrow Shares - Not Amounts

Debt is tracked as **shares**, not asset amounts. Here's why.

If the pool tracked raw amounts, it would need to update every borrower's balance every second interest accrues. With thousands of accounts, that's impossible.

Instead:

- When you borrow 1,000 USDC, you receive **borrow shares** (initially equal to 1,000 shares)
- Your share count never changes
- As interest accrues, `total_borrows` grows - making each share worth more USDC

```
Your borrow balance = your_shares × total_borrows / total_shares
```

**Example:**
```
Borrow 1,000 USDC  →  receive 1,000 borrow shares

30 days later, 50 USDC interest has accrued:
  total_borrows = 1,050  |  total_shares = 1,000

Your balance = 1,000 × 1,050 / 1,000 = 1,050 USDC owed
```

Your shares stayed at 1,000. The debt grew automatically - without touching your record.

## Interest Accrual - Lazy Update

The pool does **not** accrue interest on every block. It uses a lazy pattern - interest is calculated on-demand.

Before every deposit, withdrawal, borrow, or repay, the pool runs `update_state()`:

```
1. Time elapsed = current_timestamp − last_updated_timestamp
2. Rate factor  = time_elapsed × borrow_rate_per_second
3. Interest     = current_borrows × rate_factor
4. New borrows  = old_borrows + interest
5. Update last_updated_timestamp = now
```

If two transactions happen in the same ledger, `update_state()` returns immediately - there's nothing to accrue.

Between transactions, debt silently grows in the background. When a borrower eventually interacts, all accumulated interest materializes in one step.

---

## Origination Fee

Every borrow incurs a **one-time origination fee**, charged at the moment the loan is issued.

```
Fee = borrow_amount × origination_fee_rate
```

- The fee is **deducted before capital reaches the Margin Account** - the borrower receives `amount − fee`
- The fee is transferred directly to the **treasury**
- This is not ongoing interest - it's a flat cost paid once per borrow

## Utilization & Liquidity Risk

The pool tracks one key ratio:

```
Utilization = total_borrows / (available_liquidity + total_borrows)
```

At high utilization, two things happen simultaneously:
- The [Interest Rate Model](/learn/interest-rate-model) makes borrowing more expensive, pushing borrowers to repay
- Withdrawals can be **blocked** if the pool has less liquidity than the redemption amount

If utilization is at 95%, only 5% of deposited funds are available for withdrawal. An LP trying to redeem more than that will get an `InsufficientPoolBalance` error. They must wait until a borrower repays.

This is the primary risk LPs accept - not credit risk (that's handled by liquidations), but **liquidity risk** at high utilization.
