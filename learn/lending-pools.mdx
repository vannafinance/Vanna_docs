## The Prime Brokerage Model

The best way to understand Vanna's pools is through the lens of a **prime brokerage**.

A prime broker provides a single credit line to a fund manager. The fund deploys that capital into equities, derivatives, or any market — all from one account. The prime broker doesn't care what individual trades are running. It monitors the fund's *overall* collateral and acts only if the aggregate position becomes unsafe.

## The Core Idea

Every borrow on Vanna comes from a Lending Pool.

A Lending Pool is a smart contract that holds a single asset — USDC, XLM, or EURC — and makes it available for Margin Accounts to borrow. Liquidity providers deposit into the pool, earn yield from borrower interest, and can withdraw at any time.

The pool doesn't pick strategies. It doesn't manage risk. It just holds capital, lends it out, and collects interest. The pool earns interest on every dollar lent — regardless of what the Margin Account does with the capital.

## Two Sides

Every pool has two participants with different goals:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Liquidity Providers" icon="piggy-bank">
    Deposit an asset, receive **vTokens**, earn yield passively as borrowers pay interest. No active management needed.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Margin Accounts" icon="chart-line">
    Borrow from the pool against posted collateral. Deploy capital into DeFi strategies. Pay interest that flows back to LPs.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

LPs and borrowers never interact directly. The pool intermediates everything — collecting deposits on one side, issuing credit on the other.

## Per-Asset Isolation

Vanna runs a separate pool for each supported asset:

A USDC depositor has zero exposure to the XLM pool. Bad debt in one pool cannot affect another. Each pool carries only the risk of its own borrowers.

## Risk Isolation

The pool enforces one rule: **it only lends when the Margin Account is healthy**.

Before any loan goes out, the [Risk Engine](/learn/health-factor) checks the account's collateral value against its existing debt. If the account is near the liquidation threshold, the borrow is blocked.

If an account's health deteriorates after borrowing — because collateral dropped in value or interest accumulated — the [liquidation engine](/learn/liquidation) closes positions before the pool takes a loss.

The pool never makes credit decisions. That logic lives in the Risk Engine. The pool just executes what gets approved.
