The health factor is Vanna's safety mechanism. Every borrow and withdrawal runs through one check: would the post-operation health factor remain at or above **1.1×**? If yes, the operation proceeds. If no, it is blocked.

---

## The Formula

$$
\text{Health Factor} = \frac{\text{Total Collateral Value (USD)}}{\text{Total Debt Value (USD)}}
$$

Both values are computed live — fresh oracle prices, current borrow shares, current collateral balances — at the moment of every operation. The health factor is never stored; it is always re-derived on demand.

| Health Factor | Status |
|---|---|
| > 1.5× | Safe — comfortable buffer |
| 1.1× – 1.5× | Caution — monitor closely |
| ≤ 1.1× | Liquidatable |

## A Worked Example

A Margin Account holds two assets as collateral and has one outstanding borrow:

| Component | USD Value |
|---|---|
| Collateral asset A | $5,000 |
| Collateral asset B | $3,000 |
| **Total collateral** | **$8,000** |
| Outstanding debt | $4,000 |
| **Health factor** | **8,000 ÷ 4,000 = 2.0×** ✓ |

The account is healthy. Now suppose collateral asset B drops 80% in price:

| Component | USD Value |
|---|---|
| Collateral asset A | $5,000 |
| Collateral asset B | $600 |
| **Total collateral** | **$5,600** |
| Outstanding debt | $4,000 |
| **Health factor** | **5,600 ÷ 4,000 = 1.4×** ✓ |

Still healthy. If asset B falls to near zero:

| Component | USD Value |
|---|---|
| Collateral asset A | $5,000 |
| Collateral asset B | ~$0 |
| **Total collateral** | **~$5,000** |
| Outstanding debt | **$4,400** *(interest has accrued)* |
| **Health factor** | **5,000 ÷ 4,400 = 1.14×** — approaching threshold |

Once the ratio reaches 1.1×, the account becomes liquidation-eligible.

## How the Borrow Check Works

The health check at borrow time is different from what most lending protocols use.

**Standard protocol formula:**
$$
\frac{\text{Collateral}}{\ \text{Existing Debt} + \text{Borrow Amount}\ } \geq 1.1
$$

**Vanna's formula:**
$$
\frac{\text{Collateral} + \text{Borrow Amount}}{\ \text{Existing Debt} + \text{Borrow Amount}\ } \geq 1.1
$$

The borrowed amount is added to **both sides**. This reflects how Vanna accounts work: capital borrowed from a Lending Pool stays inside the Margin Account and is deployed into strategies — those positions then become tracked collateral in the next health check. The protocol treats the borrowed capital as immediately self-collateralizing.

The practical effect is a significantly more permissive first borrow:

| Collateral | Existing Debt | Borrow | Vanna check | Allowed? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $1,000 | $0 | $5,000 | $6,000 ÷ $5,000 = **1.2×** | ✓ |
| $1,000 | $0 | $9,000 | $10,000 ÷ $9,000 = **1.11×** | ✓ |
| $1,000 | $0 | $10,001 | $11,001 ÷ $10,001 = **1.09×** | ✗ |
| $1,000 | $800 | $300 | $2,100 ÷ $1,100 = **1.9×** | ✓ |

With zero existing debt, you can borrow up to **10× your collateral value** before the check fails.

<Info>
After borrowing, the ongoing health factor is evaluated using the standard formula — collateral value divided by total debt. The permissive borrow formula only applies at the moment of the borrow request itself.
</Info>

## What Moves Your Health Factor

| Event | Effect on Health Factor |
|---|---|
| Deposit more collateral | ↑ Ratio improves |
| Withdraw collateral | ↓ Ratio worsens |
| Open a new borrow | ↓ Ratio worsens |
| Repay debt | ↑ Ratio improves |
| Collateral asset price rises | ↑ Ratio improves |
| Collateral asset price falls | ↓ Ratio worsens |
| Borrowed asset price rises | ↓ Ratio worsens (debt costs more) |
| Time passes (interest accrues) | ↓ Ratio drifts down slowly |
| Deploy borrowed capital into a tracked position | ↑ Ratio may improve (new collateral recognized) |

Interest accrues continuously but is only written to state when a pool operation occurs. Between transactions, your debt is silently growing — even if no on-chain event has happened.

## The Three Checks

The Risk Engine runs a health check before three types of operations:

| Check | When it runs | Formula used |
|---|---|---|
| **Borrow guard** | Before any borrow is issued | `(Collateral + Borrow) / (Debt + Borrow) ≥ 1.1` |
| **Withdraw guard** | Before any collateral withdrawal | `(Collateral − Withdrawal) / Debt ≥ 1.1` |
| **Liquidation guard** | Before liquidation is executed | `Collateral / Debt ≤ 1.1` (account must already be unhealthy) |

If an account has no debt, the withdraw guard is skipped entirely — there is no risk to check.

---

## Why 1.1× and Not 1.0×

A threshold of 1.0× would mean an account becomes liquidatable exactly when debt equals collateral — leaving no surplus to cover the cost of unwinding the position. By the time a liquidation executes, a price movement could leave the protocol with unrecoverable losses.

The 1.1× buffer (10%) provides:

- **Headroom for costs** — there is always a surplus available to cover the cost of closing positions
- **Grace for volatility** — a brief oracle spike to 1.09× does not immediately result in liquidation; the account needs to remain below threshold
- **Predictability** — traders know exactly when their position is at risk and can manage leverage accordingly
